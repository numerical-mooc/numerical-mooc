You can expect lots of enthusiasm for the subject. We are four professors who believe in the power of open-source software, enjoy numerical computation and think that Python code is beautiful. And we want to share it with you.

We want to share what we know, and what we don't know—the latter is an invitation to learn together. You can't expect us to know everything! In fact, in relation to the vast amounts of knowledge out there, we may know very little indeed. But we know how to learn, connecting things we read with things we find online and things our _friends_ may know. Learning is connecting all those possible things: people, concepts, artifacts, ideas.

Because _learning is connecting_, you can expect us to be present, interacting, participating. You can expect us to participate in discussion boards, throw tweet-ups, post our thoughts on blogs and publish our code on GitHub. We won't limit ourselves to the course platform; we'll use any platform that we find useful to make connections.

You can't expect us to answer individual email messages, though. That platform is not one that we can manage very well, unfortunately. We are deluged by email messages that arrive in a never-ending stream and get buried in our respective Inbox. Believe us when we say, you don't want to be one of those neglected senders. 

Here, in the course platform, you have our attention. On Twitter, you can use the hashtag [#numericalmooc](https://twitter.com/hashtag/numericalmooc) to talk about the course, and sometimes you'll see us appear in the wingding.
