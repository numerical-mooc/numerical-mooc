The topic of numerical methods is fundamental in graduate science and engineering, and you'll find a similar course in any good graduate program. The contents are often similar: techniques for devising algorithms to solve differential equations and the analysis of these techniques in regards to their approximation qualities. In this case, we are connecting—via this MOOC—three such courses:

1. [Prof. Lorena A. Barba teaches](http://lorenabarba.com/) MAE 6286 Numerical Techniques in Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering at the George Washington University in the United States.
1. [Prof. Carlos Jerez](http://www.ing.puc.cl/cuerpo-docente/jerez-carlos/), at Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile, teaches IMT2100 Aplicaciones de Análisis Funcional y Ecuaciones Diferenciales Parciales en Ingeniería.
1. [Dr. Ian Hawke](http://www.southampton.ac.uk/maths/about/staff/ih3.page) is co-Director of the Southampton Centre for Doctoral Training in [Next Generation Computational Modelling](http://www.ngcm.soton.ac.uk/). He will teach the core numerical methods course of the [program's first-year](http://www.ngcm.soton.ac.uk/programme/taught.html), NGCM6002.

The idea of connecting these courses is a unique initiative. We are collaborating in the preparation of this MOOC, discussing and agreeing on content and organization, and at the same time connecting our student groups as a community. Our goal is to create a high-quality course and a motivating learning experience.
